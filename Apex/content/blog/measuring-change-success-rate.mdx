Change management teams love dashboards. Pie charts showing change type distribution, bar charts of changes by department, trend lines tracking volume. Most of it is noise.

Here are the five metrics that actually matter — and what to do when they trend the wrong way.

## 1. Change Success Rate

**Definition:** % of implemented changes that achieved their intended outcome without causing an unintended incident.

**Why it matters:** This is your north star. Everything else is in service of this number. A declining success rate tells you something in your process is breaking down — whether that's inadequate testing, poor risk assessment, or approvers not reading the change requests.

**Target:** 95%+ for most organisations. World-class teams hit 99%.

**What to do if it's declining:**
- Review failed changes for patterns (specific teams, change types, time of day)
- Check whether risk scoring is calibrated correctly
- Look at rollback plan quality — are they specific and tested?

## 2. Mean Time to Approval (MTTA)

**Definition:** Average time from change request submission to final approval.

**Why it matters:** Change management that's too slow creates pressure to bypass the process. Teams will submit emergency changes to skip the queue, or simply deploy without a change record. MTTA tells you where the bottleneck is.

**Target:** Varies by change type. Standard changes: < 4 hours. Normal changes: < 48 hours.

**What to do if it's high:**
- Identify which approvers are the bottleneck
- Check whether changes are being submitted with incomplete information (forcing back-and-forth)
- Consider async review for the CAB instead of synchronous meetings

## 3. Emergency Change Rate

**Definition:** % of all changes classified as emergency.

**Why it matters:** Emergency changes bypass standard process by design. A small emergency rate (2–5%) is normal. Above 10% is a signal that either the emergency criteria are too loose, or the standard process is too slow and teams are gaming it.

**Target:** < 5% of all changes.

**What to do if it's high:**
- Audit recent emergency changes — did they meet the actual criteria?
- If criteria are being gamed: tighten the definition and add a post-hoc review requirement
- If teams are being forced to use emergency because standard is too slow: fix standard

## 4. Change Failure Rate by Category

**Definition:** Success rate broken down by change category (infrastructure, application, security, etc.) and by risk score.

**Why it matters:** Aggregate success rate hides important patterns. If your infrastructure changes succeed 99% of the time but your application deployments fail 20% of the time, you have a specific problem to solve — not a general one.

**Target:** No single category should be consistently below 90%.

**What to do if a category is underperforming:**
- Review testing requirements for that category
- Check whether the risk scoring model accurately captures risk for that type
- Talk to the teams submitting those changes — often they know exactly what the problem is

## 5. Backlog Age

**Definition:** Average age of pending changes waiting for review or approval.

**Why it matters:** A healthy backlog turns over quickly. Changes waiting more than a week are either blocked on an approver, missing information, or stuck in a poorly designed workflow. Old backlog items often get approved in bulk under time pressure — increasing risk.

**Target:** 80% of pending changes less than 72 hours old.

**What to do if backlog is aging:**
- Identify which stage changes are stalling at (submission, review, approval, scheduling)
- Send automated reminders to approvers with pending items
- Check for changes that are "waiting on submitter" — often forgotten after initial submission

## What Not to Measure

A few things that look like useful metrics but aren't:

**Total change volume.** More changes aren't better or worse. Volume without context is noise.

**Changes by department.** Useful for capacity planning, not quality management.

**CAB attendance rate.** If you're measuring whether people showed up to the meeting, you've already lost.

**Time in CAB meeting.** Optimise for decisions made, not time spent.

## Building Your Dashboard

Start simple. Track success rate and MTTA in a weekly report. Add emergency rate and backlog age once you have baseline data. Category breakdowns come last, when you have enough volume per category to be statistically meaningful.

The goal isn't a beautiful dashboard — it's a process that consistently delivers safe, fast change.
